How it works
Network-connected WiFi IR blasters — point an IR device at one instead of this remote's own built-in IR when it needs to reach a closed cabinet or another room. No Home Assistant required. See astrion-ir-extender for the firmware.

Extender

Both are on the extender's own web page — open http://<its-ip>/ in a browser and look for "Mac Address". The MAC is what keeps this extender's identity stable: IR devices point at it by an id derived from the MAC, so you can rename it, move it to a new IP, or even remove and re-add it later without breaking every device that uses it.

Apple TV

How it works
Control an Apple TV directly over your network — no Home Assistant and no Harmony hub needed. Each paired Apple TV shows up as a media_player entity (for example media_player.appletv_salon) that any media player / source card, button or hotkey can use, and the Apple TV remote card can target it too. Your phone or this remote must be on the same network as the Apple TV.

Android TV

How it works
Control an Android TV / Google TV (NVIDIA SHIELD, Chromecast with Google TV, Sony, Philips…) directly over your network, like the Google TV app does — no Home Assistant and no ADB needed. Each paired TV shows up as a media_player entity (for example media_player.androidtv_shield) plus a remote entity, usable by the TV remote card, media player / source cards, buttons, hotkeys and Activities.
Title, cover and any app: install the optional Astrion TV Helper on the TV (not on the Play Store): download astrion-tv-helper.apk, then follow the wiki steps (ADB install + two permission lines). Open it on the TV, then click + Helper next to the TV and type its code.

Samsung TV (Tizen)

How it works
Control a Samsung TV with Tizen (2016 onwards) directly over your network, like Samsung's SmartThings phone app — no Home Assistant. The TV asks once to allow the remote. Each TV shows up as a media_player entity (for example media_player.samsung_salon) with its apps, volume and power, plus a remote entity for the TV remote card, buttons, hotkeys and Activities.
Turning it on: a TV in standby with its network on gets its power key; one fully off is woken with Wake-on-LAN. On the TV, turn on Power On with Mobile (Settings › General › Network › Expert Settings; the place changes with the year). A wired connection is the most reliable.

Add a Samsung TV

Turn the TV on, then enter its IP address (TV settings › General › Network › Network Status, or your router's list). The TV then asks to allow the remote: choose Allow with the TV's remote.

Sky Q / Sky+ HD

How it works
Control a Sky box directly over your network, like Sky's own phone app — no Home Assistant, no pairing. Each box shows up as a media_player entity (for example media_player.sky_salon) plus a remote entity for the TV remote card, buttons, hotkeys and Activities. A Sky Q also says whether it's on or in standby, the channel and the programme on now (with its picture, from Sky's guide); its favourite channels are the Sources. A Sky+ HD only takes the remote's keys.

Add a Sky box

Turn the box on, then enter its IP address (Sky Q: Settings › Setup › Network; or your router's list). The remote finds out whether it's a Sky Q or a Sky+ HD.

LG TV (webOS)

How it works
Control an LG TV with webOS directly over your network, the way LG's own phone app does — no Home Assistant. The TV asks once to accept the remote. Each TV shows up as a media_player entity (for example media_player.lgtv_salon) with its inputs, apps, volume and power, plus a remote entity for the TV remote card, buttons, hotkeys and Activities.
Turning it on: the TV is woken with Wake-on-LAN. On the TV, turn on Turn on via Wi-Fi (or Mobile TV On) in Settings › General › Devices (the name changes with the webOS version). A wired connection is the most reliable.

Add an LG TV

Turn the TV on, then enter its IP address (TV settings › Network, or your router's list). The TV then asks to accept the remote: accept it with the TV's remote.

Zidoo / Eversolo

How it works
Control a Zidoo media player (Z9X, Z10, Z20, UHD3000, UHD8000…) or an Eversolo audio streamer (DMP-A6, DMP-A8…) directly over your network, like Zidoo's own phone app does — no Home Assistant, no pairing. Each player shows up as a media_player entity (for example media_player.zidoo_z9x) with what it plays, plus a remote entity, usable by the TV remote card, media player / source cards, buttons, hotkeys and Activities.
Turning it on: a Zidoo that is fully off is woken with Wake-on-LAN, which needs a wired network connection and "Wake on LAN" allowed in the player's settings. Older models (such as the Z9S) can't be woken over the network: use an IR code for power instead.

Add a Zidoo or an Eversolo

Turn the player on, then enter its IP address (Zidoo settings › Network, or your router's list).

Denon / Marantz receiver

How it works
Control a Denon or Marantz AV receiver directly over your network, like their own phone app — no Home Assistant, no pairing. Each receiver shows up as a media_player entity (for example media_player.denon_salon) with its power, volume, mute, inputs (with the names you gave them) and sound modes, plus a remote entity for buttons, hotkeys and Activities. It uses the receiver's web API, so it works next to Home Assistant's Denon integration. On a receiver with HEOS, what HEOS plays (title, cover, play / pause, next) and its favorites come with it.
Turning it on: set the receiver's Network Control to Always On (Setup › Network; on a Marantz, Auto-Standby / Network Standby), so it answers while in standby.

Add a receiver

Enter the receiver's IP address (Setup › Network › Information, or your router's list). The remote finds its model and the port its web API answers on.
